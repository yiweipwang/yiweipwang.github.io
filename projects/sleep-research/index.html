Smartphones and adolescent sleep

Everyone agrees phones at night are bad for teenagers' sleep. This project asked the more useful question: which specific behaviors do the damage, and which are noise?

Year
2023
Type
Health research
Methods
Literature synthesis, data analysis
Output
Research paper

The question

"Screen time" is a blunt variable. An adolescent passively watching videos at 9pm, one messaging friends at midnight, and one woken by notifications at 3am all log "phone use," but the mechanisms — light exposure, arousal, sleep interruption — are different, and so are the likely effects. The goal was to separate those mechanisms and identify which usage behaviors actually predict degraded sleep quality and the long-term health outcomes that follow from chronic short sleep.

The approach

I synthesized findings across sleep-medicine, psychology, and public-health literature, then compared behavioral predictors — timing of use, interactivity of use, and in-bed versus pre-bed use — against reported sleep outcomes such as sleep latency, duration, and daytime functioning. Working across fields meant reconciling studies that measured "sleep quality" in incompatible ways, which became a project of its own.

What I took from it

This was my education in the unglamorous core of research: definitions. Most of the disagreement in the literature dissolved once studies were grouped by how they operationalized "phone use" and "sleep quality." It permanently changed how I read abstracts — the methods section first, conclusions second.
